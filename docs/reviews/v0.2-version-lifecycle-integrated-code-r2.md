## Nonblocker findings

1. **The stage-5 time check is 300s stricter than the design, and the stage-6 check is missing** (1749-1753).
   - The check measures against `work`, but the design's 600s (Linux) / 1140s (macOS) are relative to runtime end; the 840s provider check measures against `end` too.
   - On macOS this needs at least 1440s left after stages 1-4, which leaves about 350s for them. That's plausible but tight, and it can only cause a false red.
   - There is no stage-6 entry check (480s / 1020s).
   - Fix: compare against `end`, and add the RejectC entry check.
2. **The negative pair can expire before its cleanup.** Its restart (1905) caps daemon lifetime at `work`, but its sealed cleanup only runs in the final cleanup after the provider stage. If the macOS provider uses its whole budget close to `work`, the daemons exit on their own. `prepare` then fails and the run is a false red with the workspace kept. With finding 1's stricter check this is unlikely in practice, but the two are coupled. Fix: seal the negative pair right after `restored_b_loaded`, before the provider.
3. **Provider failure labels aren't fully mapped.** About a dozen labels the supervisor can actually emit are missing from `report_failure` (provider 108-124), so they collapse to `ChildFailed` and lose their cleanup facts. Examples: `lifecycle_provider_deadline` (keychain_check 574), `owned_account_setup_deadline`, `owned_account_already_exists`, `owned_home_already_exists`, `interpreter_changed`, `owned_account_report`, `child_cleanup_incomplete`, `operation_input_limit`. The run stays red, but diagnosis suffers and the workspace is kept. Fix: map the full closed set, and add a Python↔Rust label-parity test.
4. **The rollback-order tests from my design constraint 9 are only partly there.** `cleanup_allowed` and `qualification_complete` are tested as pure functions. Nothing locks "RecoverB never prepares" or "exactly one prepare per positive pair" at the orchestration level, so that is verified by inspection only, until the hosted run.
5. **Minor:**
   - The A status check doesn't do the canonical-bytes comparison that `running()` does; the sealed revalidation covers this.
   - A pre-launch time shortfall is recorded as `Inputs`, not `Timeout`.
   - A signal arriving after `signal_task.abort()` (1992) is silently swallowed. The window is tiny.
   - `Pair::start` (1692, 1831) still caps lifetime at `end`.
